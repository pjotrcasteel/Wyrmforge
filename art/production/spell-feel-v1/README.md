# Spell motion, identity and rank studies

The player should feel upgrades through gesture, release, projectile character and impact, as well as damage. Injury is visual only and never changes movement, cast release, recovery eligibility, damage, cooldown or input handling. These files are art studies; they do not modify gameplay.

## Current implementation

Open `index.html` over HTTP to compare ranks I–III for all eight spell identities, including healthy/hurt/critical mage poses. `spell-rank-review.gif` compares the four starter spells across three ranks. The same analytical mage and staff are used throughout. The spell gesture is a skeletal overlay: root and legs are untouched, and the free arm temporarily takes casting priority over the injury guard. Release starts at the supplied cast event with zero added delay. Recovery is a visual envelope, not an action lock.

`spell_profiles.mjs` keys visual identity by SpellId, with an independently named gesture, silhouette, rank signature and recoil profile. Schools are shared design families, not the animation lookup key. The existing eight spell definitions each have their own profile. Twenty-four existing evolutions have separate identity routes checked against the C# catalog. These evolution routes are names for later art production; the current gallery renders base spell ranks, not finished evolution effects.

For rank upgrades, emphasize a clear primary silhouette, a concentrated release, and a short readable impact. Keep rank I recognizably the same spell as rank III. Reserve split projectiles, wide novas, different delivery patterns and other semantic changes for upgrades/evolutions that actually provide them. In the review, target spacing and effect scale are arranged for side-by-side inspection; they are not world-space hitbox declarations.

## Sound and further asset work

Procedural sound sketches are now generated by `spell_audio.mjs`; final recorded/designed production sounds remain pending. Arcane: contained pressure and a low resonant release. Fire: a short combustion thump with a dry trailing roar. Frost: a hard crystal snap and granular break. Storm: a sharp crack with a brief electrical tail. Higher ranks should add low-frequency weight, transient layers and richer material response while preserving the spell's identity; do not simply increase volume. Use distinct sounds for release and confirmed contact.

Final work still includes painted sprites/VFX, production sound assets, evolution-specific effects, eight-direction casting renders, multi-spell visual budgets and live event integration. Do not delay an automatic cast to accommodate an anticipation animation. If a future spell is explicitly a charged ability, its charge belongs to its gameplay definition and its event timeline. Actual damage, projectile count, hit radius, chain targets, release times and status effects must come from the simulation. Art profiles must not create those mechanics.

## Validation and reproduction

Run `node check_spell.mjs`. It compares profile/evolution IDs to the existing C# catalogs and checks 13,824 pose cases across eight spells, three ranks, three injury states, 32 locomotion phases and six cast times. It checks unchanged root/leg positions, unchanged skeletal lengths, finite coordinates, zero added release delay and source-pose immutability. `validation.json` records the measurements.

Run `node render_review.mjs /path/to/scratch-frames` with `@napi-rs/canvas`, then encode at 30 fps. The 96-frame GIF spans three complete walk strides and two review casts. Browser and native-canvas renderers use the same `drawSpellPanel` and skeletal `applyCastPose` functions.

Primary reference: Riot's [VFX style guide](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2017/10/dev-leagues-vfx-style-guide/) and [public guide PDF](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/VFX_Styleguide_final_public_hidpjqwx7lqyx0pjj3ss.pdf), focusing on shape, value, timing and clarity. WyrmForge retains its own restrained dark-fantasy art direction. Canonical local references are `art/README.md`, `art/world/forest-monastery-encounter-v1.png` and the earlier mage identity study.


## Gesture, impact and sound revision

Each gesture now has its own recovery envelope and a distinct release pose for ranks I, II and III. Frost cuts across its release, Fire rebounds through a heavier thrust, Storm snaps and settles quickly, and Arcane holds its compressed posture longer. A bounded blend returns the arm smoothly to its locomotion/injury pose; this never locks an action. Projectile origin is captured on the release frame instead of following the later moving hand.

Contact effects differ by material: Arcane fragments collapse inward, Fire emits one combustion beat followed by upward embers, Frost throws angular crystal splinters, and Storm produces a dry contact snap. Mastery blast/nova cues remain tied to the appropriate base-spell rank in this review. Evolution route names still need their own authored effects.

`spell_audio.mjs` synthesizes eight sound identities across three ranks, with separate release/contact sketches (48 distinct sample buffers). Higher ranks add body/partials/layers with the same bounded single-voice peak rather than a rank volume multiplier. The browser starts muted and unlocks sound only when the listener presses Sound on. It plays the selected rank so the three displayed rank examples do not create three overlapping casts. Pausing, muting or changing selection stops scheduled sounds. Injury state does not select a weaker sound or later release.

`spell-sound-review.mp4` compares ranks I and III for the four starter spells, with sound, in a 12.8-second sequence. `render_sound_review.mjs` produces native-canvas frames and a deterministic PCM track. Encode frames at 30 fps using H.264/yuv420p and AAC; use `-movflags +faststart` for the review clip. The video has 384 frames and an audio track. These are synthetic sound sketches and analytical motion/VFX, not final shipped artwork.

`check_feel.mjs` verifies finite repeatable sound buffers, separate release poses per rank, WAV headers, 48 distinct sound sketches, bounded peaks and 1,152 stable launch-origin cases. `feel-validation.json` records results. `check_preview.mjs` exercises real viewer logic with native Canvas at widths 342 and 960: initial mute, user sound activation, rank and spell switching, one sound pair per cast, and mute. This is a canvas-adapter test, not a real-browser audio-output test. The existing skeletal tests still check 13,824 pose cases with unchanged root/legs.

## Focused primary and larger motion revision

The focused-magic direction uses one primary spell at a time. Choosing a spell through a level-up draft replaces the primary and keeps learned knowledge/ranks available for combination skills. A separate Frostfire skill combines the effects into one projectile: it freezes and burns, without independently firing an ice bolt beside a fire bolt.

`focused-magic-review.mp4` compares Fire Bolt I, Fire Bolt III and Frostfire in 4.8 seconds, with sound. The casting silhouette now includes a larger torso recoil, a raised staff hand and a visible staff arc about the unchanged right-hand grip. The casting arm sweeps independently; root and legs remain unchanged. This is a motion sketch, not final character art. Review clips are paced for comparison, not a replacement for gameplay cooldowns.

The viewer's Combination selector previews Frostfire on either eligible host. The primary keeps its geometry and receives a combined fire/crystal accent. This preview does not create a second projectile or a separate secondary cast clock. The gameplay implementation is being reviewed separately on `gameplay/focused-magic-v1`, with a real single-primary scheduler and captured combination/status payloads. Earlier clips in this folder record previous motion revisions; this focused review is the latest comparison.
