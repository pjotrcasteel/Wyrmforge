## LOCKED — one reusable engine per responsibility (owner decision, 27 October 2026)

**Architectural invariant for all future WyrmForge changes:** maintain **one shared, extensible engine per responsibility**, not one implementation per creature, spell, arena, school or scene. A single engine may have specialized profiles, modules, render backends and platform adapters; it does **not** mean one giant class or a new catch-all runtime. New content supplies assets, typed configuration and adapters, rather than copying loops or creating feature-specific engines. Before accepting a new system, inventory the existing owner, reuse/extend its contract, and add cross-content regression coverage.

**LOCKED — 2.5D pixel-first across every engine (29 October 2026):** Every engine and its lab must serve the same **mobile-first 2.5D pixel-art runtime**: C# gameplay/AI/AR use real ground-plane topology and optional elevation; animation presents authored directional sprites/layers; G lights sprite/material/occluder surfaces; FXP and VFX display bounded 2.5D pixel effects; audio/haptics attach to the same world events. **Runtime 3D meshes, full 3D worlds or skeletal mesh pipelines are not prerequisites for any engine or milestone.** Offline Blender/3D sources may optionally produce approved pixel sprites (including existing Ashfang/Hollowed work); they must not pull the runtime toward 3D. The closed AN0–AN6 experiment remains archived for optional future study only; its rejected model, advanced GPU source-skin and source biomechanical solvers are not active animation prerequisites. Require a demonstrated 2.5D limitation, mobile visual/performance comparison and owner approval before adding a mandatory 3D dependency. This applies to ENG, G, AI, sprite animation, FXP, VFX, AUD and AR milestones, subject to the explicit 30 October AN6 closure below. See the [cross-engine contract](architecture-and-quality.md#locked--pixel-first-25d-runtime-contract-29-october-2026) and [asset route](art-and-animation.md#pixel-first-authoring-and-gameplay-output--locked-29-october-2026).

**Cross-engine event rule (non-negotiable):** a confirmed Fireball impact, kill or destructible transition is **one C#-authorized gameplay event**, with stable identity, timing, positions/directions, damage/element traits, affected entities and bounded effect parameters. The shared lighting, AI-observation, animation, visual-physics, VFX and audio systems may independently **consume** that event through versioned, typed adapters. Their visual or sonic reactions must never re-decide combat, loot, targeting, nav blocking or game state; no duplicate explosion decisions or per-spell cross-engine orchestration. A common event is not a requirement for each subsystem to run at the same tick rate, nor permission for JS to own gameplay truth. Specify pause, resume, despawn, replay, duplicate-event and reduced-effects semantics.

**Modularity and performance:** one reusable contract does not prohibit scene-specific art, enemy-specific behavior, elemental profiles, separate render backends, or intentionally staged legacy fallbacks. It prohibits **parallel authoritative engines** and unbounded per-feature pipelines. Provide mobile budgets, culling, LOD, pooling, fallbacks, deterministic tests, device captures and instrumentation for any new engine capability. Refactor in behavior-preserving vertical slices; never replace working assets or the accepted painted Town just to satisfy an abstraction.

### LOCKED — actual 2.5D pixel-art acceptance for every visual milestone (29 October 2026)

The game we are building is **handcrafted, Gothic, mobile-first 2.5D pixel art**, with a consistent three-quarter/top-down camera, readable silhouettes, grounded scenery, organic movement and dramatic but restrained magical lighting. The references are an **art-direction benchmark**, not assets to copy or evidence that WyrmForge already meets the target. Every relevant **G, AN/ANIM, AR, FXP, VFX and ENG** change inherits the following exit conditions, in addition to its own gameplay, CI and device gates.

| Non-negotiable visual contract | Implementation and measurable evidence before milestone completion |
|---|---|
| **Pixel grid and authored scale** | Record an intended **game-pixel-to-world-unit scale** for actors, terrain, props and effect atlases; preserve native silhouette/pixel clusters under the actual camera. Verify crisp hard-pixel assets with appropriate atlas sampling, pixel-safe filtering, snap/transform choices and atlas edge padding; prevent crawling/shimmer, texture bleed, subpixel halos and accidental smoothing. Allow intentionally soft *lighting*, mist or water responses without filtering away the authored art. Reject an arbitrary high-resolution 3D-look character simply downscaled to match. |
| **One perspective, grounded depth** | Use shared camera/projection, authored feet/root anchors, Y/depth ordering and optional C# height/occlusion semantics. Show a mage/enemy walking **in front of and behind** a tree, ruin and tall prop, with ground contact and no floating sprite, false collision or foreground masking through the actor. Geometry used for a GPU shadow must not silently become gameplay collision. |
| **Sprite/material alignment** | A rendered actor or prop uses its **matching frame/direction/pose** for albedo, authored normal, roughness, emissive and depth/caster data where supplied. Validate alpha cutout, premultiplication/edge colors, normal-space conventions and atlas UVs. Missing optional maps use an explicit generic fallback; no misaligned injured-body material or duplicate painted illumination. Source-skin candidates share the same world/contact/render material contracts and do not become mandatory for other actors. |
| **Lighting and effects respect pixels** | The **single shared G engine** lights ground, characters, foliage, water and world effects from the same authoritative sources. Verify moving/extinguished Fireball plus fixed emitter, true shadow occlusion, night/zero-light invariants, readable telegraphs and no full-bright sprite or double Canvas night mask in the GPU path. Effects may glow or use smooth falloff, but cannot erase silhouettes with full-screen blur, plastic gloss or screen-wide orange wash. |
| **Cohesive authored assets and geography** | AR geography remains genuine C# topology; detailed art is **not** a substitute for AR0–AR3 solid-color acceptance. AR4–AR5 enforce consistent tile/object scale, authored Gothic palette, biome-specific flora/stone, subtle natural transitions, grounded props and no repeating mirrored tiles or inconsistent art perspectives. Preserve the original approved painted 2D Town, separately from the Hunt GPU cutover. |
| **Live mobile proof and regression** | Provide **actual running-game** 320px and 390px portrait screenshots/video, at normal gameplay framing plus inspected native pixel crops, from the same representative Wet Forest scene. Include mage, Hollowed/Ashling, rooted foliage, wet ground, one stationary light, moving Fireball and real occlusion; compare Canvas/default versus optional GPU/AN paths where applicable. Test camera travel, animation, dark frame, multiple lights, reduced-effects mode, pixel sampling, input/telegraphs and deterministic C# replay. Real iOS/Android frame pacing, memory, overdraw and thermal acceptance is a **separate measured gate**; desktop emulation alone does not complete it. |

**Milestone stop rule:** A green test suite, beautiful standalone concept/reference, Blender turntable, lit isolated lab, or flat-color geography proof **is not** a visually accepted playable 2.5D Hunt. Require truthful artifacts linked to the PR, visual owner approval where specified and no regression in current fallback/live game. Never implement another renderer, animation engine, lighting stack or 3D asset requirement to pass these gates. Do not revive closed [AN0–AN6 PR #387](https://github.com/pjotrcasteel/Wyrmforge/pull/387) or interrupt [G4/G5 PR #381](https://github.com/pjotrcasteel/Wyrmforge/pull/381) or [AR1 PR #404](https://github.com/pjotrcasteel/Wyrmforge/pull/404) from this roadmap clarification.

#### P1 remaining slice: targeting breakable loot-bearing props (recommended; tuning pending playtest)

**Why:** `RunSimulation.Aim.cs::PrimaryCandidates()` currently yields only enemies and the active Wyrm, while `ArenaEnvironmentState` already supports real physical barrels/crates, projectile impacts, salvage drops and LOS. Right-side tap-to-point expires after **350 ms**, so tapping a crate can fail if a spell is still cooling down.

**Recommended input hierarchy:** **active deliberate manual aim** (existing continuous drag/mouse/right-stick) **→ explicit tap/click on a valid breakable prop** (temporary C#-owned `prop id` focus, not just a fixed aim angle) **→ automatic hostile acquisition** **→ nearby eligible breakable props only if no hostile target is legal** **→ existing no-target behavior**. On clicking/tapping a hostile, retain the current attack/manual semantics rather than implicitly replacing all controls. The lock ends when the object breaks, leaves range/LOS, becomes unavailable, the Hunt changes/pauses, or the player explicitly changes aim. A suggested short inactivity limit is **3 seconds**, subject to combat testing; an active cooldown must not make the tap silently disappear before the next cast. Hostile acquisition always pre-empts *ambient* prop targeting, but not deliberate player focus.

**Touch ergonomics:** a **stationary tap** that hits an actual prop (using a modest accessible hit area at 320/390px) can focus that object; dragging continues to mean the existing movement or manual-aim stick on its established side. The current 55% split must not steal the movement finger. Desktop click-release over a crate may focus; click/hold and dragging elsewhere retain deliberate manual aim. Do not add a mandatory extra aim button or a permanent lock-on that forces movement toward the object. Give the focused crate a restrained, unmistakable ground/outline cue, not a giant marker.

**Authority and fairness:** keep prop identity **typed separately from enemy IDs**, even if integers coincide. C# validates accessibility, radius, line of sight, health and actual spell/projectile impact. A prop must **never** outrank an active dangerous enemy under ordinary auto-aim; excluded scenery and non-destructible loot chests are interaction/proximity objects, not free combat targets. Preserve projectile wall collision, encounter pacing, mobile controls and once-only drop integrity. Add real Hunt 320/390px input tests for motion, enemy proximity, obscured crates, cooldown, prop break/loot and target reset. **This is a recommendation recorded for subsequent implementation, not a claim it has been implemented or finally playtest-approved.**

## 14. Definition of success and Claude kickoff

An ordinary first-time player can start either the optional story or free exploration, see readable Gothic map/environment, learn an initial school, **auto-aim and auto-cast at legal enemies by default with optional fully manual aiming whenever desired**, survive a short coherent Wyrm Hunt, extract genuine material, research a real mutation without destructive RNG, use the unified Nexus to prepare a meaningful build and visibly improve it in a real combat run. They eventually meet equivalent shared endgame eligibility regardless of story choices, while their private spells remain private unless published with consent.

A competitive player can run **Seasonal Expedition** (routes/knowledge/strategy) or **Seasonal Arena** (generated wave survival) under the same frozen four-week rules, retry freely, have the best **independently verified** result placed on distinct boards, and use earned powerful permanent rewards through the Nexus to push further in their **Eternal Hunt**.

An Eternal player can save at a **secured refuge**, quit, relaunch the **town** (not a battle), use **Continue Eternal Hunt**, return to the **same refuge, same world, same committed routes**, suffer recoverable death setbacks and build a lifelong verified frontier without arbitrary seasonal wipe.
